Section 01

What the system already understands

A single working session was spent reading everything available about the unit and building a structured engineering model of it. What follows is the result of that session — not a projection of what might be possible later.

The starting material was four documents: a research proposal, a technical report, a technical presentation, and the electrical construction drawings. None of them contained a process flow diagram or P&ID for the machine being built. The process was reconstructed instead from the instrument tag numbering inside the electrical package — the tag sequence encodes the flow path, and the control system descriptions name every major item of equipment.

The process

A recirculating batch cavitation upgrader. Feed is heated above its pour point, pumped at pressure through a cavitation device where collapsing vapour bubbles crack heavy hydrocarbon molecules, then either returned for another pass or routed to product. Sonic and electrical energy are injected into the device to stabilise the cracked fragments.

The recycle branch is the heart of the process. A charge circulates through steps 2 to 5 repeatedly until it meets specification, which means the number of passes — not the flow rate — is the primary operating variable, and the one that most directly determines both product value and throughput.

The machine and its instrumentation

Skid-mounted, split into an electrical room and a process room. Two variable-speed pumps, three tanks, four control valves, an electrically heated feed tank, and a dedicated cooling circuit for the sonic system.

The control system carries 17 continuous measurements and 36 discrete signals — six flow meters with totalisers, pressure and temperature both entering and leaving the cavitation device, an inline viscosity transmitter on the outlet, tank levels, and gas detection. Nine spare measurement channels are already wired and terminated.

Why this matters

The viscosity transmitter is the most valuable instrument on the skid — it is the natural measure of whether a batch is finished, and the natural target for optimisation. The flow meters are sufficient to track material around the recycle loop and count passes. This is a better-instrumented unit than most of its size, which is precisely what makes systematic improvement possible rather than theoretical.

Section 02

Where production is currently left on the table

This machine has one dominant operating decision, and it pulls against itself.

Every additional pass through the cavitation device makes the oil lighter and more valuable. Every additional pass also burns energy, occupies the machine, and consumes equipment life. More passes means better oil and less of it.

Illustrative, not measured. The shape is characteristic of this kind of process — value rises with diminishing returns while cost rises steadily, so margin peaks and then falls. Where the peak actually sits for this machine on this feedstock is precisely what a commissioning test campaign establishes.

Short of the peak, oil leaves the plant worth less than it could have been. Past it, machine time and power are being spent producing quality that does not earn its cost.

The peak is not a fixed setting. It moves — and a unit run on habit cannot track a target that moves.

Nobody knows where that point sits for this machine on this feedstock, because it has never been measured. Establishing it and keeping it current is the largest recurring opportunity the unit presents — it repeats on every batch, for the life of the equipment.

The same measurements answer the throughput questions too: where the batch cycle loses time, which step limits the rate, and what a second unit should be built to do differently.

Section 03

The compounding loop

The value is not a one-off analysis. It is a cycle that tightens each time it runs — and it is the mechanism behind everything in the following three sections.

Step 01
Measure
Every batch logged in full — conditions, pass count, energy, outcome.
Step 02
Find the pattern
Which variables actually moved the result, separated from noise.
Step 03
Change one thing
A single deliberate adjustment, with the expected result written down first.
Step 04
Compare
Outcome against prediction. Where they differ, the model was wrong and improves.
↺  each pass through the loop makes the next recommendation sharper

Most units never close this loop. They accumulate operating data for years without a structure that turns it into knowledge, and improvement stays a matter of opinion.

The difference between a machine that has run for a year and a machine that has learned for a year is entirely in whether this loop was running.

Section 04

Short term · the first months of operation

Horizon one
Make the machine legible

Before anything can be improved, what the machine actually does has to be established as fact rather than expectation.

  • Characterise the baseline — the documented reference every future change is measured against. Without it, no improvement can ever be proven, only asserted.
  • Make product value visible in real time. The unit measures viscosity continuously but cannot measure the gravity the oil is actually sold on. Laboratory samples taken during commissioning would establish the relationship between the two, turning an existing instrument into a live readout of what the batch is worth.
  • Measure the uplift-per-pass curve — how much each additional cycle genuinely delivers, and where the returns flatten. This is the curve that answers the question in Section 02.
  • Find where the batch cycle loses time and which step limits throughput.
  • Verify inherited performance expectations against this feedstock, replacing assumption with measurement.
  • Check instrument integrity continuously — drift and faults caught early, before they quietly corrupt months of conclusions.
Section 05

Long term · ongoing operation

Horizon two
Operate at the most profitable point, continuously

Once the curves are measured, the optimum can be calculated rather than guessed — and recalculated as conditions move.

  • Set pass count against margin, not habit. The point where an additional pass stops paying is computed from measured data and updated as inputs change.
  • Track the market. When the heavy-to-light price differential widens, additional processing pays; when it narrows, it does not. Operating targets that move with the market rather than lagging it.
  • Adapt to feed variability — recommended treatment matched to the quality of the oil actually arriving, rather than fixed to an average that may rarely occur.
  • Raise throughput by removing the limiting step, identified from cycle data rather than from impression.
  • Predictive maintenance. Cavitation is inherently erosive. Tracking device condition over time converts unplanned failures into scheduled work.
  • Autonomous monitoring — routine analysis running on its own against live data, surfacing only what needs a decision.
  • Design V2 from measured evidence rather than from opinion, so the next unit inherits what was learned instead of what was assumed.
Section 06

Scaling · multiple units

Horizon three
Turn one working machine into a repeatable product

Selling or replicating units requires something a single working machine does not automatically produce: a design that can be handed to someone else.

  • Produce the documented, transferable design package. A machine cannot be sold, warranted, or manufactured repeatably from a design that exists only as one physical object. Assembling that package is a direct output of the work described above.
  • Generate performance evidence that survives technical diligence — measured, sourced and reproducible, so that what is presented to a buyer or partner holds up when examined.
  • Establish the performance envelope across feedstocks, so what can be promised to a customer is known rather than hoped.
  • Standardise commissioning, so each new unit is brought online against a proven protocol instead of being worked out again from scratch.
  • Monitor a fleet. Comparing units against each other turns every installation into evidence about all of them — and a problem found on one becomes a fix applied to all.
  • The accumulated engineering record becomes an asset in itself — part of what a buyer, partner or lender is actually being offered.
Section 07

The capability grows as the system becomes better understood

Everything above is written from a standing start — four documents, no operating data, and a machine that has not yet run.

The current model was built entirely from paper. Forty questions about the unit remain open, and they are the ordinary kind: what the equipment is rated for, what the instruments actually measure, what the incoming oil looks like. As those are answered, and as the machine starts producing live data, both the depth of the model and the range of what can be done with it expand together.

The relationship is direct. Better information produces sharper analysis; sharper analysis produces better questions; live operating data converts what is currently reasoning about a machine into measurement of one. The capability described in the preceding sections is therefore a floor rather than a ceiling.

Section 08

Yellow flags found in the documents

These surfaced while reading the existing material. None is presented as a conclusion, and none is fatal — but each is the kind of item that is inexpensive to address now and awkward to discover later.

FLAG 01The quoted performance figures rest on an unmatched sample comparison

Tracing each headline performance figure back to its source shows they are reproduced only by comparing two laboratory samples that are not a matched before-and-after pair — a feed sample from one date against a product sample taken three months earlier from a different batch. The documents do contain a genuine matched pair, taken from the same oil on the same day, but it is not cited anywhere.

MeasureAs quotedMatched pair
Sulphur reduction32%14%
Product API gravity15.29.0
Product viscosity8,900 cSt354,000 cSt

This is not a finding that the technology does not work. The matched pair shows real, physically coherent upgrading — gravity rose, sulphur fell, heavy residue converted from 65% to 60%, and the oil measurably lightened. The effect is genuine. It is roughly half of what has been quoted, and it was measured on a different feedstock than this unit will process.

The reason to note it now is that the same check would be run by any buyer, lender or partner conducting technical diligence, using only documents the business already holds. Establishing a measured performance basis on the actual feedstock resolves it entirely.

FLAG 02The sonic system has no monitoring of any kind

The sonic energy input is central to how the process is understood to work. In the electrical design it appears as two standard receptacles and a dedicated cooling circuit, and it is not connected to the control system — there is no measurement of its power, frequency or condition.

If it degrades or fails, nothing reports it. The first symptom would be batches quietly failing to reach specification, noticed late, with no way to identify the cause. A modest addition would close this.

FLAG 03Two documents disagree on how sulphur leaves the process

The technical narrative states that sulphur is converted into a harmless solid. The laboratory data included in the same document does not support this. Separately, both the earlier research proposal and the current electrical design specify equipment for handling hydrogen sulphide — meaning two independent engineering parties, years apart, designed for its presence.

This is a straightforward question to settle definitively before the unit runs, and it affects gas handling and personnel protection.

FLAG 04A measurement capability is installed and going unused

The variable-speed drives carry an unused output signal, and the control system has spare input channels already terminated. Connecting them would make energy consumption per pass measurable — a required input to the profitability question in Section 02.

No new equipment is involved. This is the least expensive improvement identified.

All four are ordinary findings for a technology at this stage, and all four are cheap to resolve while the machine is still being finished. They are included here because identifying this kind of item early — before it reaches a customer, a lender, or a commissioning failure — is a representative example of what the work in the preceding sections produces.
